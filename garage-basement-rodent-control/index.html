Why garages attract rodents.

A garage offers rodents shelter, stored cardboard and fabric for nesting, and often food, whether that is pet food, birdseed, or items stored from a pantry overflow. Attached garages near older parts of Vero Beach sometimes have gaps around the door seal or where the structure meets the house that give mice and rats easy access.

Detached garages and storage sheds common on larger Vero Beach lots see the same pressures, often with fewer barriers between the structure and surrounding yard, canal edge or open ground.

What we check in a garage or storage area.

  • Door seals and weatherstripping where the garage meets the exterior.
  • Stored boxes, fabric and cardboard for nesting material.
  • Wiring and stored electronics for chewing damage.
  • Gaps where the garage structure meets the main house.
A garage is a house with fewer barriers, and rodents notice.

Treating an active garage problem.

Traps and stations go along wall lines and behind stored items where droppings and gnaw marks show real activity, not scattered across open floor space. If pet food or birdseed is stored in the garage, switching to sealed containers is a simple step that removes an ongoing food source while treatment is underway.

Basements, storage rooms and lower levels.

Fewer Vero Beach homes have a true basement given the region's water table, but lower-level storage rooms, garages built partially below grade, and similar spaces see comparable issues, particularly moisture that draws rodents seeking a stable, hidden environment away from Florida's summer heat.

Protecting stored belongings and wiring.

Chewed wiring in a garage, whether it is a car charger, stored electronics, or the garage's own electrical run, raises a real fire risk beyond the cost of replacing the item. Our rodent damage repair service handles that kind of chewed wiring or material once the active rodent problem is cleared.

Sealing the garage itself.

Once activity is addressed, sealing focuses on the door seal, any gap where the garage meets the main structure, and foundation vents if the garage has them. Our rodent exclusion service covers this sealing work as part of the same visit when both are needed.

Seasonal storm supplies and garage clutter.

Garages in Vero Beach often hold hurricane preparation supplies, generators, extra fuel containers, plywood, and stored water, much of it sitting untouched for long stretches between storms. That kind of undisturbed storage is exactly what gives rodents an opportunity to nest without being noticed for weeks at a time.

Periodically checking behind and underneath stored storm supplies, even outside of an active season, catches early activity before it becomes an established problem by the time the next storm actually arrives and the supplies get pulled out again.

Shared garages in townhome communities.

Townhome and some condo communities in Vero Beach have garages that share a wall or ceiling void with a neighboring unit's garage, similar to the shared-wall issue that can affect living space in these buildings. If garage activity keeps returning after treatment, a shared void with a neighboring garage is worth considering as a possible explanation.

Garage door seals.

A worn garage door seal is one of the most common entry points we find in Vero Beach garages. Florida heat and sun exposure break down rubber seals faster than in cooler climates. We check seal condition as part of every garage visit.

Storage habits that make a difference.

Cardboard boxes stored directly on a garage floor give rodents both shelter and nesting material. Switching to sealed plastic bins, especially for anything containing food or fabric, removes much of that appeal. This is a simple change most homeowners can make right away.

Basements and slab homes.

Most Vero Beach homes sit on a slab rather than a full basement, but some properties do have partial below-grade space. When that is the case, we check the same entry points, gaps around utility lines, foundation cracks, vent openings, that apply to any ground-level structure.

Attached garages connect directly to living space through an interior door, which means a garage problem can spread indoors quickly if the connecting door does not seal well. We check that seal too.

A garage used as a workshop or extra storage area tends to accumulate more clutter over time, and that clutter often becomes the first place we check for signs of activity.

We check both the interior and exterior of a garage during a visit, since entry points are often visible from outside even when the activity itself is noticed inside.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Do garages need different treatment than the rest of the house?

The approach is similar, inspection, trapping and sealing, but garages often have specific issues like door seals and stored nesting material that get extra attention.

Should I throw out cardboard boxes in my garage?

Switching stored items to sealed plastic bins removes nesting material rodents favor, which helps even before treatment starts.

Can rodents damage a car parked in the garage?

Yes, our vehicle and car rodent removal service covers wiring and cabin damage specifically.

Do detached sheds need the same treatment as an attached garage?

Yes, any structure with signs of activity gets included in the inspection and treatment plan.

What if I store pet food in the garage?

Switching to sealed containers kept off the floor removes an ongoing food source that draws rodents back even after treatment.
