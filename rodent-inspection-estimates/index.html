Why the inspection comes first.

A number given before anyone has looked at the property is a guess, and guesses do not hold up once the actual scope turns out larger or smaller than expected. Instead, a technician walks the interior and exterior, attic, crawl space if there is one, garage and yard perimeter, before recommending anything. That walk-through is what a plan and a price are actually based on.

For a Vero Beach address, that usually means checking the roofline and soffits for roof rat access, the yard perimeter and any canal or fence line for Norway rat burrows, and interior spaces like the kitchen, pantry and garage for mouse activity. Each property gets checked on its own terms rather than a single fixed routine applied regardless of what it actually looks like.

What the inspection covers.

  • Attic, soffits and roofline for roof rat or mouse entry points.
  • Crawl space and foundation edges for gaps and moisture damage.
  • Garage, shed and stored belongings for signs of nesting.
  • Yard perimeter, fence lines and any canal or ditch edge for burrows.

Droppings, gnaw marks, grease smears and nesting material each tell a technician something different about what species is active and how long it has likely been there. Fresh droppings look darker and softer than old ones, and a heavier concentration in one area usually points to where the animals are actually nesting rather than just passing through.

The property tells us the plan, not a phone guess.

The written estimate.

After the inspection, we walk through findings and put a written estimate in your hands before any further work begins. That estimate breaks down what was found, what it will take to address it, trapping, sealing, cleanup or repair, and the cost for each piece, so nothing about the bill is a surprise partway through the job.

When a standalone inspection makes sense.

Some calls are for an inspection alone, without committing to treatment yet, which is a reasonable way to understand the scope of a problem before deciding how to proceed. A property changing hands, a landlord checking a rental between tenants, or a homeowner who has heard noises but has not seen a rodent directly are all common reasons to book an inspection on its own.

What to have ready for the visit.

Mentioning when you first noticed activity, which rooms or areas are involved, and whether you have seen droppings, chewing or an actual animal helps a technician plan the visit before arriving. Access to the attic and any crawl space matters too, so let us know ahead of time if either one needs a ladder or key we should bring.

After the inspection.

If treatment is recommended, the next steps usually pull from mouse control, rat control, rodent exclusion, or a combination depending on what the inspection found. You decide what to move forward with once you have the full picture, not before.

Inspections for property transactions.

Buyers, sellers and landlords in Vero Beach sometimes request a rodent inspection as part of a property transaction or a change in tenancy, separate from an active complaint. This kind of inspection follows the same thorough process as any other, checking the same likely entry points, and results in the same written summary of findings.

For a rental property specifically, documenting the condition at move-in or move-out with an inspection report can help clarify responsibility if a rodent issue comes up later in the tenancy, though we do not offer legal guidance on how that documentation should be used.

What you receive after the inspection.

You get a written summary of what we found, where we found it, and what we recommend. This includes photos when they help illustrate an issue. You can review it at your own pace before deciding how to proceed.

No pressure to decide on the spot.

We do not require an immediate decision after an inspection. Take the estimate, ask questions, and call back when you are ready. Rodent problems in Vero Beach rarely resolve on their own, but a short delay to review your options is reasonable.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

Does the inspection cost extra beyond treatment?

Ask about current pricing when you call. The important part is that you see the full plan and cost before any treatment work begins.

Can I book just an inspection without treatment?

Yes, a standalone inspection is available if you want to understand the scope of a problem before deciding how to proceed.

How long does an inspection take?

It depends on the size of the property and how many areas need checking, but a technician takes the time needed to look at every likely entry point rather than rushing a quick glance.

What if the technician does not find anything?

We tell you that directly. Not every call turns up active rodent presence, and an honest inspection sometimes rules it out rather than manufacturing a job.

Do you inspect rental properties between tenants?

Yes, landlords and property managers use an inspection to check a unit before or after a tenant turnover.
