Keeping Mice Out of a Vero Beach Garage Before Hurricane Season

Heavy rain and flooding during hurricane season displace outdoor rodents toward higher, drier shelter, and a garage is often the first stop before they reach the house itself.

Why garages are a common storm-season entry point.

A garage sits closer to ground level than most of the house, often has a door seal that wears down over time, and provides warmth, shelter and stored materials that make it attractive to a mouse displaced by flooding or heavy rain. Checking a garage before hurricane season begins addresses the most likely entry point before pressure increases.

What to check before the season starts.

  • The rubber seal along the bottom of the garage door for gaps or wear.
  • Weatherstripping around any side door into the garage.
  • Vents or utility penetrations where pipes or cables enter.
  • Stored cardboard boxes that could become nesting material.

Why plastic bins matter more than people expect.

Cardboard boxes stored long-term in a garage are exactly the material mice look for when building a nest, and a garage full of cardboard gives a displaced mouse both shelter and building material in one place. Switching to sealed plastic bins before hurricane season removes that draw regardless of what happens with the door seal itself.

Food storage during storm prep.

Storm preparation often means extra stored food and water in a garage, and unsealed packaging is an easy target for mice looking for a food source during a period of displacement. Keeping storm supplies in sealed containers rather than their original cardboard or thin plastic packaging reduces this risk during exactly the period when pressure is highest.

What to do if you find activity after a storm.

If a storm passes and you notice new droppings, gnaw marks or nesting material in the garage, mouse control addresses the active animals directly. A rodent inspection identifies exactly which entry point saw the activity, so it can be sealed properly rather than guessed at.

Sealing for the long term, not just one season.

Our rodent exclusion service uses hardware cloth, metal and proper door seals rather than temporary fixes, since a garage checked once before a single storm season still needs the same entry points holding up through the next one. A yearly check ahead of hurricane season is a reasonable habit for any Vero Beach garage.

A quick pre-season checklist.

Walk the garage's perimeter from outside looking for daylight showing through any gap around the door or foundation. Check that stored food and boxes are in sealed containers, and note anything that looks worn or damaged. Address it before the season's first named storm arrives.

Generators and fuel storage.

A generator and stored fuel containers, common storm preparation items in a Vero Beach garage, sit untouched for most of the year outside of active storm response, which gives mice plenty of undisturbed time nearby if the surrounding area is not periodically checked. Wiring on a generator itself is not immune to gnawing damage either, so a quick visual check before storm season begins is worth the few minutes it takes.

After the storm passes.

Once a storm has passed and normal routines resume, take the opportunity while unpacking storm supplies to look for any new droppings, gnaw marks, or nesting material that were not there before the storm. This is also a reasonable time to check that door seals and vent screens held up against wind and rain, since storm conditions can loosen or damage the same seals meant to keep rodents out.

A garage that made it through a storm season without issue still benefits from the same annual check the following year. Conditions change, seals wear, landscaping shifts, a roof gets repaired, enough from one year to the next that last year's clean result says little about this year.

A simple pre-season checklist.

Check the garage door seal for gaps. Look at vent screens for tears. Confirm stored food is in sealed containers. Walk the exterior wall for daylight showing through any crack. This takes under fifteen minutes and catches most obvious problems.

Storm shutters and rodent access.

Storm shutters, when stored rather than installed, sometimes create small gaps behind them where they lean against a garage wall. Check behind stored shutters occasionally, since this is an easy spot to overlook.

A quick summary.

Check seals, vents, and stored food before storm season begins. Recheck after any storm passes, since wind and rain can create new gaps.

Attached versus detached garages.

An attached garage connects directly to your living space, so a gap there puts the whole house at risk, not just the garage. A detached garage is more contained, but still worth the same level of attention since stored items and vehicles are just as exposed.

Setting a yearly reminder.

Since storm season returns every year, setting a calendar reminder for a pre-season garage check is a simple way to make sure it does not get overlooked during busier preparation tasks like stocking supplies or reviewing your evacuation plan.

Coordinating with neighbors before a storm.

Storm preparation often means neighbors are all out checking their own properties around the same time. It is a natural moment to mention any shared fence line or drainage concern that might affect both properties once the weather arrives.

Making it part of your routine.

Storm preparation already involves checking supplies, shutters, and generators. Adding a five-minute rodent check to that same routine costs almost nothing extra and can prevent a much bigger cleanup after the season ends.

A garage that stores emergency supplies deserves the same attention as one that stores everyday items, since mice do not distinguish between the two when looking for shelter.

A checklist taped inside a garage cabinet is a simple way to make sure the same steps happen every year, even if a different household member handles storm prep.

A few minutes of checking now is far easier than dealing with a rodent problem in the middle of storm cleanup later.

Common questions

Does hurricane season really increase mouse activity in garages?

Yes, flooding and displacement from outdoor burrows during heavy rain often pushes mice toward higher, drier shelter, including garages.

Is a worn door seal really enough to let a mouse in?

Yes, a mouse only needs a gap about a quarter inch wide, and a worn or gapped door seal easily provides that.

Should I switch all my garage storage to plastic bins?

It is a reasonable step, since cardboard is exactly the nesting material mice look for, especially before a period of higher displacement risk.

What if I already have activity before the season starts?

Address it now rather than waiting, since an existing gap will see more pressure once storm season begins.
