What rodent damage typically looks like.

Rodents chew for two reasons: their teeth grow continuously and need wearing down, and gathering material for a nest. That means wiring, ductwork, insulation, drywall and even structural wood can all show damage in a home with an established rodent problem, and the extent varies enormously depending on how long the activity has been going on before it was noticed.

Older Vero Beach homes near downtown and Central Beach, with original wiring and ductwork in some cases, tend to show more extensive damage when a problem has gone unaddressed for a while compared to newer construction with less to chew through.

Common repair categories.

  • Chewed electrical wiring, a genuine fire hazard beyond the repair cost.
  • Damaged HVAC ductwork, which affects cooling efficiency.
  • Compressed or soiled attic insulation needing replacement.
  • Gnawed drywall, soffits and exterior trim.
Repairing damage without removing the cause just invites more of it.

Repair only follows removal and sealing.

Repair work happens after rodent trapping and removal and rodent exclusion are complete, since repairing damage while rodents still have access just means doing the same work again later. We coordinate the sequence so repair is the last step, not a rushed first response.

Electrical work and outside trades.

Chewed wiring that affects active electrical circuits goes to a licensed electrician rather than getting patched informally, since faulty repair work on wiring is its own fire risk. Findings and damage get identified and documented here, with next steps coordinated for anything outside this scope of work.

Ductwork and cooling efficiency.

Chewed ductwork is easy to overlook since it usually sits above a ceiling or in a crawl space, but a breach lets cooled air escape into an attic instead of the living space, raising cooling costs during Florida's long, hot season. A rodent inspection that includes a duct check catches this before it shows up as an unexplained spike in a utility bill.

Documentation for insurance.

We document findings and repairs with notes and photos, which is useful if you plan to file a claim with your homeowners insurance, though coverage for rodent damage varies by policy and is worth confirming with your insurer directly.

Repairing soffit and roofline damage.

Roof rats gnawing at a soffit vent or roofline gap over time can widen a small opening into a significant repair job if left unaddressed, since the damage compounds every time an animal reuses and enlarges the same route. Repairing this properly means matching materials to what will hold up against both weather and future gnawing attempts, not just patching the visible hole.

Homes with wood soffits, more common in some of Vero Beach's older neighborhoods, need different repair materials than a home with vinyl or aluminum soffit panels, and we match the repair to what is actually there rather than a one-size approach.

Insulation replacement.

Chewed or soiled insulation loses much of its effectiveness. We assess insulation condition as part of any damage repair estimate. Replacing it is often included alongside structural repair when the two areas overlap.

Drywall and trim repair.

Gnawed drywall corners and chewed baseboard trim are common in homes with an established rodent problem. We patch small areas ourselves and can coordinate larger drywall sections with a contractor. Trim replacement is usually a same-visit fix.

A written scope before any work begins.

Before repair work starts, we walk you through exactly what needs attention and why. You see the scope in writing before agreeing to anything. This avoids surprises once the work is underway.

Repair paired with exclusion.

Repairing damage without sealing the entry point that caused it tends to invite a repeat problem. We pair damage repair with rodent exclusion whenever an active entry point is found. This protects the repair work you are paying for.

Some repairs are cosmetic. Others affect a home's structure or safety. We tell you clearly which category any given repair falls into so you can prioritize accordingly.

Electrical damage gets flagged as a priority ahead of cosmetic issues, since chewed wiring carries a real fire risk that should not wait for a convenient time.

We keep you updated as repair work moves forward, especially if an outside contractor becomes involved for electrical or larger structural items.

We can also advise on preventing future damage to the same area once repairs are complete, so the fix holds up over time.

We can also patch small exterior holes left from removed nesting material, so the repair looks finished rather than leaving a visible gap behind.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Can you repair chewed wiring yourselves?

Active electrical wiring repair goes to an outside licensed electrician, since that work requires separate licensing.

Does homeowners insurance cover rodent damage?

Coverage varies by policy. We provide documentation of findings and repairs that you can bring to your insurer to ask directly.

How do I know if my ductwork has rodent damage?

A noticeable rise in cooling costs or uneven cooling in parts of the house can point to a duct breach, which a rodent inspection can check directly.

Will you repair damage before the rodents are removed?

No, repair follows confirmed removal and sealing, since repairing an active entry point just means redoing the work.

Do you repair drywall and soffits too?

Yes, once removal and sealing are complete, we patch gnawed drywall, soffits and exterior trim as needed.
