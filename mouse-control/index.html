What a mouse call usually looks like.

Most mouse calls start the same way. Someone hears light scratching in a wall or the attic after dark, or finds small pointed droppings in a kitchen drawer or along a garage shelf. House mice are small enough to slip behind cabinets and inside wall cavities, so the noise is often louder than the animal causing it. On the call, tell us the room, the time of day you notice it, and whether you have seen droppings or gnaw marks.

Vero Beach's older homes near downtown and Central Beach tend to have more entry points around aging window trim, vent screens and where utility lines pass through a foundation. Newer construction closer to Florida Ridge is tighter, but garage door gaps and dryer vents still give mice a way in during cooler stretches of the year.

How we find where mice are traveling.

A technician checks the attic, crawl space if there is one, garage, kitchen and exterior perimeter for droppings, gnaw marks, grease smears and gaps large enough for a mouse to pass through. Mice leave a fairly obvious trail once you know what to look for: a run of small droppings along a baseboard, a chewed corner on stored cardboard, a greasy smudge where fur brushes a wall on the same route night after night.

  • Kitchen and pantry cabinets, especially where plumbing lines pass through the back wall.
  • Attic insulation and the underside of the roof deck near soffit vents.
  • Garage corners, stored boxes and areas behind parked cars.
  • Utility penetrations for water, gas and cable lines at the foundation.
Removal without sealing only resets the clock.

Trapping and removal.

We place snap traps and tamper-resistant stations along the runs we find, matched to how mice actually move rather than scattered at random. Placement near the wall line, behind appliances and inside cabinets where droppings show up gets results faster than traps set in the middle of open floor space. We check placements on a schedule until activity stops, and we explain what is set and where before we leave, especially if there are children or pets in the home.

Closing the entry points.

A mouse only needs a gap about a quarter inch wide, roughly the width of a pencil, to squeeze through. That means gaps around pipes, vents, garage door bottoms, and where siding meets the foundation are all candidates. We seal these with hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar rather than foam alone, since foam does not hold up to repeated gnawing. Our rodent exclusion service covers this sealing work in more detail if a whole-house pass is what you need.

After the visit.

We return to check traps and confirm droppings have stopped appearing. If you keep hearing activity after a visit, that usually points to a gap we have not sealed yet rather than a failed treatment, so tell us exactly where the noise is coming from and we go back to that spot first. Our rodent inspection and estimates visit is also available on its own if you want a full picture of entry points before committing to ongoing work.

How long mouse control usually takes.

A single mouse with one clear entry point can sometimes be resolved in one visit, especially in a newer, tightly built home near Florida Ridge. A more established problem, with multiple entry points or several weeks of activity already behind it, usually needs a follow-up visit or two to confirm the seals hold and no droppings reappear. We set expectations on timeline during the first visit rather than promising a single-visit fix before we have actually inspected the property.

Preventing mice from returning after a visit.

Once entry points are sealed, the ongoing steps that keep mice from finding a new way in are mostly about maintenance. Keep vegetation trimmed back from the house, store pet food and pantry items in sealed containers, and check weatherstripping on doors each season before the weather turns. None of these steps replace a proper inspection and sealing job, but they extend how long the results last.

Multi-unit and shared-wall buildings.

Condos and townhomes near Central Beach that share a wall or an attic space with a neighboring unit sometimes see mice move between units through a shared void. A single unit's treatment can look like it failed when the real activity is actually next door. If you live in a shared-wall building and treatment does not fully resolve the issue, mention the shared-wall layout when you call, since it may be worth coordinating with a neighbor or the HOA on a combined approach.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about mouse control

How fast do mice reproduce in a house?

A female house mouse can have a litter every few weeks under good conditions, which is why a small problem noticed early is much easier to resolve than one left for months.

Are mouse droppings dangerous?

Dried droppings can carry hantavirus and other pathogens. Do not sweep or vacuum them dry. Wet them with disinfectant first, then wipe them up wearing gloves.

Will one visit clear a mouse problem?

Sometimes, if the entry points are limited and get sealed on the same visit. Larger or older homes near downtown often need a follow-up to confirm the seal holds.

Do you use poison bait?

We favor traps and tamper-resistant stations placed out of reach of children and pets, and we explain exactly what is used and where before we leave.

Can mice come back after treatment?

Yes, if a new gap opens or an old one was missed. That is why sealing entry points, not just trapping, is part of every visit.

Do you handle mice in a car or garage?

Yes. If mice have moved into a parked car or a garage, our vehicle and car rodent removal and garage services cover that separately from indoor living space.
