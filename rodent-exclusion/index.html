Why sealing is not optional.

Removing whatever is currently active clears the property for a short while, but the gap that let the first animal in is still there for the next one that finds it. Exclusion work closes that opening for good, using materials chosen specifically because mice and rats cannot chew through them the way they can foam or thin plastic.

Vero Beach's older housing stock near downtown and Central Beach tends to have more small gaps from decades of settling, aging trim and past renovations. Newer construction closer to Florida Ridge is tighter but still has utility penetrations, garage door gaps and dryer vents that need proper sealing.

What we seal with.

  • Galvanized hardware cloth over vents, soffits and larger openings.
  • Sheet metal flashing at rooflines and eaves.
  • Mortar and masonry patch for gaps in foundations and chimneys.
  • Door sweeps and weatherstripping for garage and exterior doors.
Foam stops a draft. It does not stop a rat.

Finding every entry point.

A single sealed gap does little good if a second one is still open, so exclusion work starts with a full pass of the roofline, foundation, vents, and utility penetrations before any sealing begins. Mice need only about a quarter inch to get through, and rats about the size of a quarter, so gaps that look too small to matter often are not.

Sealing near canals and dense landscaping.

Properties near canals in Gifford and Vero Beach South or with dense landscaping close to the foundation see more ground-level entry attempts, so sealing on these lots often extends to foundation vents and lower wall gaps in addition to the roofline. Trimming vegetation back from the house is a maintenance step that extends how long the sealing holds.

Sealing after treatment, not instead of it.

Exclusion work follows rodent trapping and removal rather than replacing it, since sealing an entry point with active animals still inside does not solve anything. The two are scheduled together whenever the inspection confirms both live activity and a clear entry point.

How long sealing lasts.

Metal, mesh and mortar hold up far longer than foam, but no seal is permanent against Florida's humidity, storm exposure and ongoing settling. A yearly check during a rodent inspection catches any new gap before it becomes an active entry point again.

Sealing around pool enclosures and screened patios.

Many Vero Beach homes have a pool cage or screened patio attached to the main structure, and the point where that enclosure meets the house can create an additional gap that a whole-house exclusion pass should account for, not just the main roofline and foundation. We check this connection point as part of a full inspection when a property has one.

A properly sealed exclusion job typically includes a brief explanation of exactly what was closed and with what material. That gives you a record of the work for future reference, whether for your own notes or if a future buyer's inspection asks about prior pest work.

Materials that hold up in Florida conditions.

Humidity, salt air near the coast, and strong UV exposure all wear down cheaper sealing materials faster in Vero Beach than in a drier climate. We use materials chosen to hold up under these conditions rather than generic caulk or foam that degrades quickly outdoors.

Vents, screens, and utility penetrations.

Dryer vents, soffit vents, and the small gaps around cable or plumbing lines are common entry points we check during exclusion work. Each one gets appropriate hardware, not just sealant, since sealant alone often fails at a vent opening exposed to airflow and weather.

Why exclusion is not a permanent seal against every future gap.

A home settles and shifts over time. New gaps can open years after an exclusion visit, particularly after a storm or foundation settling. We are upfront about this rather than implying a single visit permanently seals a home against every future possibility.

We recommend a periodic check-up, especially after a major storm, to confirm sealed points are still holding up as expected.

We photograph each sealed point before and after the work, giving you a simple visual record of exactly what was addressed on your property.

We are glad to walk you through exactly what was sealed and why, so you understand the work done on your home rather than just receiving a bill.

We stand behind the sealing work we perform and will come back to address any point we sealed if it fails within a reasonable window.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Why not just use spray foam?

Foam blocks a draft but a rat or mouse can chew through it within days. We use metal, mesh and mortar because rodents cannot chew through those.

How many entry points does a typical home have?

It varies widely. Older homes near downtown often have more than newer construction, which is why the inspection covers the whole exterior rather than one spot.

Does sealing happen before or after trapping?

Both are usually scheduled together once the inspection confirms active animals and a clear entry point, so sealing does not trap anything inside.

Will sealing affect attic ventilation?

No, we use hardware cloth over vents rather than sealing them shut, which keeps airflow while blocking rodent access.

Does Florida humidity affect how long sealing lasts?

Metal and mortar hold up well, but an annual check catches new gaps from settling or storm exposure before they become active entry points again.
