Two different rats, two different habits.

Roof rats climb well and travel along power lines, fences and tree canopy, so they tend to enter a house at the roofline, an attic vent or a gap where a branch overhangs the roof. Norway rats stay lower, favor burrows along canal banks, fence lines and thick landscaping, and are more likely to be found in a garage, crawl space or yard than up in an attic.

Vero Beach has conditions for both. Mature canopy near Central Beach and downtown gives roof rats a route between properties, while the canals and drainage corridors running through Gifford and Vero Beach South give Norway rats natural cover close to residential lots. Telling us which one you are dealing with, based on where you see activity, helps plan the visit before a technician arrives.

Inspecting for rat activity.

A rat inspection covers the roofline, attic, soffits and eaves for roof rats, and the yard perimeter, fence lines, sheds and any canal or drainage edge for Norway rats. Rat droppings are noticeably larger than mouse droppings and rounded rather than pointed, and rub marks along a well-used route are often heavier and more obvious than the marks mice leave.

  • Rooflines, soffits and attic vents for roof rat entry points.
  • Fence lines and burrow openings for Norway rat activity.
  • Tree limbs and utility lines that connect to the roof.
  • Sheds, woodpiles and dense landscaping near the foundation.
Knowing the species changes where we start, not just what we call it.

Trapping and removal for rats.

Rat-sized traps and tamper-resistant stations go along the confirmed travel routes, at the roofline for roof rats or along a fence line and burrow entrance for Norway rats. Rats are more cautious around new objects than mice, so placements sometimes take a few days longer to produce results, and we explain that timeline rather than promising an overnight fix.

Sealing the entry point.

A rat can push through a gap about the size of a quarter, larger than what a mouse needs but still easy to overlook at a roofline or foundation edge. We seal these gaps with metal flashing, hardware cloth and mortar, since a determined rat can chew through foam or loosely packed material within weeks. Our rodent exclusion service handles this sealing work in more depth for a whole-property pass.

Rats near canals and open ground.

Properties backing onto a canal, drainage ditch or open lot in Gifford or Vero Beach South tend to see steadier Norway rat pressure than a flatter, more built-up lot further from water. Burrow openings along the bank, sometimes only visible at low water, are a common starting point for treatment on these properties.

Rats and citrus or fruit trees.

Roof rats are drawn to citrus and other fruit trees, which are common in yards around Vero Beach given the area's citrus history. Fallen or ripening fruit left on the ground gives rats an easy food source close to the house, so clearing dropped fruit regularly is a simple step that reduces the draw even before a technician arrives.

Follow-up and confirming results.

A follow-up visit checks whether traps have gone quiet and confirms the sealed entry points are holding. Rat activity that persists after sealing usually means a second entry point was missed rather than that the treatment failed, so tell us exactly where new activity shows up and we return to that spot directly.

Rats and Florida's storm season.

Heavy rain and flooding during hurricane season can displace Norway rats from low-lying burrows toward higher, drier ground, which sometimes means a sudden uptick in yard or garage activity right after a storm passes through, even on a property with no prior rat history. Roof rats are less affected by ground flooding but may seek denser attic shelter during extended wet, windy stretches.

If you notice new rat activity shortly after a named storm, mentioning that timing when you call helps a technician connect the activity to storm displacement rather than treating it as a slower, more established problem.

Why persistence matters with rats.

Rats are cautious animals. They often avoid a new object in their path for several days before approaching it. This means rat treatment can take longer to show results than mouse treatment, even when the underlying approach is similar. We set expectations for this during the first visit.

We follow up after initial treatment to confirm activity has actually dropped, rather than assuming the first visit resolved everything.

Before we arrive

  • Keep children and pets away from any area with droppings, a nest, or a suspected entry point.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; that can spread contamination into the air.
  • Do not set out your own loose bait or poison before the visit.
  • Note when and where you have seen or heard activity so the technician can start there.

Questions about rat control

How do I know if it is a roof rat or a Norway rat?

Roof rats show up in attics and rooflines and travel along wires and canopy. Norway rats stay lower, near burrows along fence lines and canal banks. Tell us where you see activity and we plan around that.

Are rats in Vero Beach a seasonal problem?

Activity can pick up during hurricane season and heavy rain, when rats seek higher, drier ground, but calls come in throughout the year given the area's warm climate.

Do fruit trees attract rats?

Yes, especially citrus and other fruit-bearing trees common in Vero Beach yards. Clearing fallen fruit regularly reduces the draw.

Can rats chew through PVC or plastic pipe?

Yes, given enough time, which is why sealing uses metal and mortar rather than plastic or foam alone.

Is rat treatment safe near a canal or drainage ditch?

We place stations away from open water and out of reach of wildlife and pets, and explain exactly what is used and where.

Do you handle rats in a commercial building?

Yes. Our commercial rodent control service covers monitoring and treatment for shops, restaurants and offices dealing with rat pressure.
